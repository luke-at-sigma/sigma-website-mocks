01 — Campaign strategy
Who Dave is. Why it works. How to use it.
Audiences: Blair · Tariq · Amir

The human insight

Dave isn't a caricature. He's a real colleague everyone has worked with. Using his name makes the problem human and specific without being mean — Dave tried to help. The system let him down. That's an empathetic read that earns trust.

The campaign platform

"No Daves Required" does three things at once: names a universal problem, positions Sigma as the fix, and does it without a single buzzword. It works for a billboard, a badge, a subject line, and a booth. That's a platform.

The CEO bridge

"Trust layer for AI apps" = data your AI can actually trust. Dave's dashboard is why AI gives wrong answers — it's running on ungoverned, stale, shadow-IT data. Sigma fixes the source. No Daves required says that in 3 words.

The NYC lens

NYC buyers are sophisticated and allergic to corporate speak. "Dave" is specific, knowing, and slightly funny — the three things NYC audiences respond to. It respects their intelligence and earns their attention.

Do this

✓
Name the problem specifically. "Dave's numbers don't match yours" beats "data inconsistency."
✓
Be empathetic to Dave. The frame is "system failure" not "Dave's fault." He tried to help.
✓
Always render "Dave" in Sigma Blue. The color makes Dave a visual anchor across the campaign.
✓
Pair proof points with the Dave frame. "95% fewer ad-hoc requests — that's 95% fewer Daves" is more powerful than either claim alone.
✓
Stay specific. "47 tabs," "still running," "nobody knows what tab 31 does" — the details earn trust.

Don't do this

✗
Don't make Dave a villain. If the copy reads as mocking him, it alienates the reader who IS Dave.
✗
Don't add buzzwords to the Dave frame. "Dave's shadow IT workaround creates governance gaps" kills the joke.
✗
Don't over-explain. "Dave" works because it's instantly recognizable. Explaining a joke is the brand voice equivalent of a bad Slack thread.
✗
Don't force Dave into technical contexts. The Dave frame is for awareness and LOB audiences — not security or data admin docs.
✗
Don't let Dave become generic. Every Dave execution needs a concrete, specific detail. Generic Daves are lazy Daves.

Open questions
Q1 — Positioning
Do we want the Dave to be self-aware (knows he's in a Sigma ad, winks at the camera) or oblivious (sincerely defending his spreadsheets)? The former needs a comedian. The latter needs an actor.
Q2 — Solo vs. ensemble
One iconic Dave who "is" the campaign, or a rotating bench of Daves each embodying a different archetype? Solo = more cohesive. Ensemble = more social content and longevity.
Q3 — Chang conflict check
Does Chang have existing tech/SaaS commitments that would conflict? No known exclusivity blockers as of Q1 2026 — but a formal check is required before any outreach begins.
Q4 — UK distribution
If David Mitchell (UK) is pursued, does Sigma have sufficient EU/UK distribution to justify the US recognition trade-off? Or does he run in a Europe-specific cut only?
Q5 — Wain directing
If Wain talent-directs, does that create an awkward dual role in production? Has real value (reduces director cost, better self-direction) but may need a separate director of record for deliverable accountability.
Q6 — Marrs positioning
Marrs is the non-Dave in a Dave campaign — does that confuse the premise or strengthen it? "We replaced Dave with someone who actually knows data" is a strong message if handled carefully.
